The Gurten course is derived from public OpenStreetMap and swisstopo mapping. Photographs by trailnet and TRIEBHAUS informed the obstacle and signage design; those photographs are not included in the repository. See the [Gurten sources](docs/GURTEN.md).

The [El Tony pilot](docs/el-tony-pilot.md) uses the Poly Haven maps above as generic ground materials. Their exact download URLs, sizes and SHA-256 hashes are listed in [sources.json](src/MTBIndoorBern/Scene/assets/el-tony/sources.json). CC0 applies to those material assets; it does not extend to Poly Haven's website content, preview renders, logos or unrelated photographs. The [trailnet opening recap](https://www.trailnet.ch/de/aktuell/recap---opening-gurtentrail-2-0) and [TRIEBHAUS project](https://www.triebhaus.ch/projekte/gurtentrail) remain visual references only. New nearby trees and undergrowth are generated in code, rather than extracted from the photographs or third-party models.

Ride sound is original procedural synthesis generated by the project. No third-party samples or field recordings are included. The historical El Tony comparison used the application's version 1 audio graph; the Dentenberg woodland comparison retains version 2 without drivetrain sound. The current app and new exports use version 3: seven continuous environmental/friction sources plus a brief one-shot click for a host-confirmed successful shift. Continuous chain/freehub buzz remains removed. See [rendering and audio provenance](docs/render-assets.md).

The Dentenberg public ground-material fallback uses the unmodified **Forest Floor** maps above. [Its manifest](src/MTBIndoorBern/Scene/assets/dentenberg/sources.json) records the exact source URLs, official byte counts and MD5 checksums, locally computed SHA-256 checksums, dimensions and verification method. [The accompanying licence note](src/MTBIndoorBern/Scene/assets/dentenberg/LICENSE.md) links the CC0 legal code. These generic materials are separate from the owner's private video-derived ground texture; raw cycling frames, recordings and personal route data are not part of this addition.

Eiger Bike Challenge uses the organisers' downloadable route with swisstopo terrain and imagery and OpenStreetMap mapping. Event and local tourism photographs informed the summer appearance; those photographs are not distributed. Race arches, spectators and furniture are code-generated interpretations. The event name identifies the recreated course, not an endorsement. See the [Eiger sources and scope](docs/EIGER.md).

## Track Lab and additional visual resources

Track Lab uses FFmpeg/FFprobe as external tools and the Garmin FIT SDK for JavaScript. Neither FFmpeg binaries nor the SDK are included in the repository; obtain them separately and retain their licences. The local working installation uses FFmpeg 9.0.2 and Garmin FIT SDK 21.221.0. Python/Pillow is used to prepare contact sheets.

The photographic leaf-litter texture remains in the local installation and is not published. The public renderer includes a code-generated alternative. The RGBA beech-twig texture was generated with the integrated ImageGen tool. See [textures and provenance](docs/render-assets.md) for paths and the prompt. Track Lab screenshots show a woodland excerpt; the complete original videos remain local.

The public `Scene/courses/uetliberg/` folder includes the course data and a `provenance.json` file containing sources, date and a GPX hash. The planned GPX and mapping derived from OpenStreetMap are distributed under ODbL. The Dentenberg mapping assets in the table above remain local.

The [Bluetooth brake concept image](docs/images/bluetooth-brake-concept.png) was generated with the built-in ImageGen tool. It is an illustrative design study, not a manufacturer photograph, a tested device or a reproduction of a selected bill of materials. Its [generation prompt and provenance](docs/brake-concept-render.md) are included.

The [Maneta v01 realistic render](docs/images/bluetooth-brake-v01-realistic.png) is based on the owner's supplied mechanical design package. The six printed parts in its Blender reference use the supplied STL geometry; ImageGen provided the final surface and lighting treatment. The original package and Blender binaries are not redistributed here. The [source manifest, limitations and prompt](docs/brake-v01-render.md) distinguish source geometry from illustrative hardware details.
